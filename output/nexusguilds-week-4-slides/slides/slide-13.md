# Slide 13 — Exercise 4.C: Process Mapping

## What this slide says
This slide (20 minutes, done in pairs) is a workshop to map 5-10 real processes onto the four tiers of the Tool Spectrum. A worked example table has columns Process | Volume | Tier | Justification | Effort, with three sample rows: "Classify customer emails" (200/day, No-Code, Zapier handles simple classification, ~2 hours); "Generate sales proposals from CRM" (20/week, Low-Code, needs CRM integration plus branching logic, ~1 day); and "Analyse contracts vs compliance rules" (5/week, AI-Assisted Code, complex business logic plus long documents, ~3 days). Learners document each process's name, volume/frequency, recommended tier, justification, and estimated effort. The slide states this output feeds directly into Week 5's agent design exercises, and gives four decision questions under "How to Choose Your Tier": 1. Who will build it? 2. Who will maintain it? 3. How complex is the logic? 4. What scale do you need?

## Key concepts
- A structured exercise to match real work to the appropriate automation tier rather than over- or under-engineering.
- Each process is profiled by volume/frequency, chosen tier, justification, and estimated effort.
- Four decision criteria — builder, maintainer, logic complexity, and required scale — drive tier selection.
- The resulting process map is an explicit input to Week 5's orchestration and agent-design work.

## Notes for a Project Manager
This is the slide most squarely in a PM's wheelhouse. The output is a prioritised, justified portfolio of automation opportunities — essentially a lightweight intake-and-triage backlog with effort estimates attached. The four decision questions are a reusable scoring rubric: builder and maintainer speak to ownership and bus factor, complexity to risk, and scale to whether the chosen tier will hold up. Use the volume and effort columns together to spot the highest-leverage candidates (high volume, low effort) and to sanity-check the ROI of anything in the costlier code tiers. Because this map feeds Week 5, treat it as a living planning artefact, not a one-off classroom exercise.
