# Findings — Process Mapping and Choosing the Right Tier (process-mapping)

## General source(s)
- [Business process mapping — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_process_mapping) — High-level definition, purpose, and the six major steps (process identification, information gathering, mapping, analysis, develop/install new methods, manage process); history and notation (flowcharts, BPMN).
- [What Is Process Mapping? Steps, Types, Examples & Template — Asana](https://asana.com/resources/process-mapping) — Plain-language definition and a 6-step "how to create a process map" walkthrough plus the seven common process-map types (flowchart, high-level, detailed, swimlane, value stream, SIPOC, BPMN).

## Specific sources
- [How to choose the best automation software — Zapier](https://zapier.com/blog/how-to-choose-the-best-automation-software/) — Decision criteria for tooling: assess automation needs and workflow complexity, integration/tech-stack fit, scalability (SSO, permissions, pricing as you grow), must-have features, security/compliance, and trial-based testing. Covers "who builds / who maintains" and "scale" criteria.
- [Low-code vs. no-code: Key differences and benefits — Zapier](https://zapier.com/blog/low-code-vs-no-code/) — Decision criteria no-code vs low-code: who builds each (non-technical users vs developers/tech-savvy users), customization/logic-complexity ceiling, and when to choose each. Covers "who builds, logic complexity" tier criteria.
- [Select Business Processes for Automation: a Comprehensive Checklist — ProcessMaker](https://www.processmaker.com/blog/select-business-processes-for-automation-a-comprehensive-checklist/) — Criteria for prioritizing processes by volume/frequency, repetitiveness, standardization (under ~15-20% exception rate), rule-based logic, plus business-impact and implementation/feasibility considerations. Covers "evaluating process volume and frequency" and "prioritizing by volume and complexity."
- [How to decide which processes to automate: A practical guide — Qntrl](https://www.qntrl.com/blog/automate-process.html) — Decision framework weighing repetitiveness, frequency/volume, complexity/risk, customer impact, resource requirements, stakeholder input, and pilot/ROI validation. Covers "decision criteria" and "effort estimation" via feasibility + ROI.

## Key facts
- Business process mapping defines what a business does, who is responsible, to what standard, and how success is measured; its purpose is to make organizations more effective and to surface improvement opportunities. — source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_process_mapping
- The classic mapping methodology proceeds through process identification, information gathering, process mapping, and analysis (then install new methods and manage the process). — source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_process_mapping
- A practical 6-step way to build a process map: select a process, document all activities, sequence the steps, draw the flowchart with standard symbols, get team feedback, then identify improvements. — source: https://asana.com/resources/process-mapping
- Process-map types match the situation: flowchart (simple), high-level (executive), detailed, swimlane (cross-functional roles), value stream (lean/waste), SIPOC (scoping), and BPMN (automation/system integration). — source: https://asana.com/resources/process-mapping
- When choosing a tool/tier, assess workflow complexity (simple data passing vs multi-step logic), integration with your existing tech stack, estimated monthly task volume for cost planning, and scalability (SSO, permissions, pricing growth). — source: https://zapier.com/blog/how-to-choose-the-best-automation-software/
- No-code targets non-technical builders with visual, pre-built components but a lower customization ceiling; low-code requires some coding skill and suits developers/tech-savvy users when logic is more complex or deeper customization is needed. — source: https://zapier.com/blog/low-code-vs-no-code/
- Prioritize processes that are high-volume/high-frequency, manual and repetitive, standardized (ideally under ~15-20% exception rate), and rule-based with clear, consistent logic. — source: https://www.processmaker.com/blog/select-business-processes-for-automation-a-comprehensive-checklist/
- A complex process that runs only once a month has less operational impact than a simpler one that runs constantly — weigh volume × frequency, not complexity alone. — source: https://www.processmaker.com/blog/select-business-processes-for-automation-a-comprehensive-checklist/
- Decide what to automate by weighing repetitiveness/rule-based nature, frequency and volume, complexity and risk, customer impact, and resource requirements, then validate with a pilot and ROI metrics before broad rollout. — source: https://www.qntrl.com/blog/automate-process.html
